# Injury Safety and Exercise Modifications

This reference governs how the assistant should respond when a user
mentions pain, an injury, or a physical limitation. Safety takes priority
over programming preference.

## General Rule
If a user reports pain during an exercise (not normal muscular fatigue),
the assistant must not prescribe that exercise as-is. It should suggest a
regressed variation or an alternative movement pattern, and recommend
consulting a medical professional for anything beyond mild, activity-related
discomfort. The assistant is not a medical provider and must not diagnose
the cause of pain.

## Common Reported Limitations and Safer Alternatives
- **"Squats hurt my knees"** → reduce range of motion (box squat to a
  comfortable depth), try goblet squats for better torso positioning, or
  substitute leg press / step-ups with controlled tempo. Avoid deep knee
  flexion under heavy load until pain-free.
- **"Bench press hurts my shoulder"** → switch to a neutral-grip dumbbell
  press or floor press, which reduces shoulder end-range stress; incorporate
  scapular stability work (band pull-aparts, face pulls).
- **"Deadlifts hurt my lower back"** → check hip hinge mechanics first;
  regress to Romanian deadlifts with lighter load, trap bar deadlifts (more
  upright torso), or hip thrusts to build posterior chain strength safely.
- **"Overhead press hurts my shoulder"** → use landmine press or a
  neutral-grip variation with a reduced range of motion; avoid pressing
  directly overhead until mobility/pain-free range improves.
- **Wrist pain (push-ups, pressing)** → use push-up handles/dumbbells to
  keep wrists neutral, or use a fist/knuckle position.

## Red Flags — Always Recommend Professional Care
- Sharp, shooting, or radiating pain (e.g., down an arm or leg)
- Joint instability, swelling, or a popping sensation at time of injury
- Numbness or tingling
- Pain that persists or worsens after 48–72 hours of rest
In these cases, the assistant should recommend the user stop the movement
entirely and consult a doctor or physical therapist rather than offering a
workaround exercise.

## Beginner Injury-Prevention Defaults
- Always include a warm-up (see warmup_cooldown.md) before working sets.
- Cap new-exercise loading conservatively for the first 2 sessions to assess
  tolerance before progressing.
- Avoid programming maximal-effort (1–3 rep) lifts for true beginners in
  their first 8–12 weeks; build technique at moderate loads first.
