# Program Design and Training Splits

## Choosing a Split by Experience Level and Frequency

### Beginner (0–1 year training age)
- **Full-Body, 3x/week** (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri): trains every major muscle group
  each session. Best for building base strength and motor patterns with
  lower total fatigue.
- Focus on compound lifts: squat, hinge (deadlift/RDL), horizontal push
  (bench/push-up), horizontal pull (row), vertical push (overhead press),
  vertical pull (pull-up/lat pulldown).
- 2–4 sets per exercise, 8–15 reps, 60–90 seconds rest.

### Intermediate (1–3 years training age)
- **Upper/Lower Split, 4x/week**: two upper-body days, two lower-body days.
  Allows more volume per muscle group per week than full-body while still
  permitting 48+ hours recovery.
- 3–5 sets per exercise, mixed rep ranges (5–8 for strength emphasis, 8–12
  for hypertrophy emphasis), 90–120 seconds rest on compound lifts.

### Advanced (3+ years training age)
- **Push/Pull/Legs (PPL), 5–6x/week**: separates by movement pattern to allow
  high per-muscle volume and frequency (2x per week per pattern if run twice
  in a week).
- Requires more careful fatigue management: deloads every 4–6 weeks,
  auto-regulation (adjusting load/volume to daily readiness).

## Time-Constrained Programming
- **2 days/week available**: full-body sessions are non-negotiable; skipping
  to a split with only 2 sessions per week under-trains most muscle groups.
- **20–30 minutes per session**: prioritize 1 compound lift + 2 accessory
  movements per session over long isolation-heavy sessions; reduce rest
  periods to 45–60 seconds where safe.

## Equipment-Constrained Programming
- **Dumbbells only**: replace barbell squat with goblet/dumbbell squat,
  barbell bench with dumbbell press, barbell row with single-arm dumbbell row.
- **Bodyweight only**: emphasize tempo manipulation, unilateral variations
  (Bulgarian split squat, single-leg RDL), and higher rep ranges (12–20) to
  compensate for lower absolute load.
- **Resistance bands**: useful for accessory/activation work and travel
  programs, but should not be the sole stimulus for advanced lifters chasing
  strength goals.

## Muscle Group Focus Requests
When a user asks to focus on a specific muscle group (e.g., "glutes," "arms"),
add 2–4 extra targeted sets for that group per week on top of the base split,
rather than removing other muscle groups from the program entirely — full-body
balance still matters for injury prevention and posture.
