# Warm-Up and Cool-Down Protocols

## Why Warm Up
A proper warm-up raises muscle temperature, increases blood flow, primes the
nervous system, and rehearses the movement pattern before loading it heavily.
Skipping it is one of the most common causes of avoidable injury, especially
for beginners jumping straight into heavy compound lifts.

## General Warm-Up Structure (8–12 minutes)
1. **Light cardio (3–5 min)** – brisk walk, bike, or rower at low intensity to
   raise core temperature.
2. **Dynamic mobility (3–5 min)** – leg swings, arm circles, hip openers,
   bodyweight squats, band pull-aparts. Avoid static stretching before heavy
   lifting; it can temporarily reduce force output.
3. **Movement-specific ramp-up sets** – for the first exercise of the day
   (e.g., squat, bench, deadlift), perform 2–4 warm-up sets working up from
   ~40% to ~85% of the working weight before the first working set.

## Cool-Down Structure (5–10 minutes)
1. **Light cardio (2–3 min)** – gradually lower heart rate.
2. **Static stretching (5–7 min)** – hold each stretch 20–30 seconds for
   major muscle groups worked that session. This is the appropriate time for
   static stretching, unlike pre-workout.
3. **Breathing/parasympathetic reset** – slow diaphragmatic breathing to shift
   out of a sympathetic (fight-or-flight) state, especially after high-intensity
   sessions.

## Special Considerations
- **Beginners**: extend the dynamic mobility phase; unfamiliar movement
  patterns need more rehearsal reps at very light load.
- **Cold environments**: extend general cardio warm-up by 2–3 minutes.
- **Joint pain history (knees, shoulders, lower back)**: add targeted
  activation work (e.g., banded lateral walks for knees, scapular retractions
  for shoulders, bird-dogs for lower back) before loading those areas.
